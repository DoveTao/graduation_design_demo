# RESULTS360 experiment narrative

## Thesis-ready English paragraph
We evaluate our 360DVO experiments on the DSET2C canonical 360DVO split, which uses a sequence-level train/val/test partition and explicitly forbids random pair splitting. Legacy `scene01` workflows are retained only for diagnostics and are not part of the main experimental protocol. As an external legacy reference, T57b represents a recovered arbitrary ERP image-pair baseline rather than a canonical-sequence-trained model, and it shows weak translation generalization, with a signed translation-direction mean of about `111.96 deg`, anti-parallel rate `0.6747`, translation-magnitude median ratio `0.1752`, and path ratio `0.1488`. In contrast, our self-developed `TRAIN360C` hybrid spherical pose baseline is trained on the DSET2C canonical train split and achieves much stronger test translation behavior, improving the signed translation-direction mean to `54.96 deg`, reducing the anti-parallel rate to `0.2157`, raising the translation-magnitude median ratio to `0.7693`, and increasing the path ratio to `0.5889`. We also reproduced the HKUST official `360DVO` baseline as `BASE360C` by repairing the `cuda_ba` extension build and import path, passing the official smoke test, and running canonical val/test inference with full sequence coverage. `BASE360D` then recovers aligned pair-level component metrics by post-processing those official trajectories against the canonical manifest. The derived test values remain very weak compared with `TRAIN360C` (`signed_tdir_mean ≈ 128.40 deg`, `anti_parallel_rate ≈ 0.8149`, `tmag_median_ratio ≈ 0.0399`), but the comparison is now evaluation-aligned rather than blocked by missing metrics. The official public demo still yields valid trajectory-level metrics, including a test path ratio of `0.043618`, and the image-directory path required a `0.5x` input adapter to avoid full-resolution OOM. A further caveat is that `TRAIN360C` still exhibits a strong val/test discrepancy in signed translation direction, with about `111.86 deg` on val versus `54.96 deg` on test, indicating that translation robustness and split consistency remain open issues.

## Concise bullet-point version
- Dataset protocol: DSET2C canonical 360DVO split, sequence-level split, no random pair split, legacy `scene01` downgraded to diagnostics only.
- T57b: recovered legacy arbitrary ERP image-pair baseline; weak translation generalization; test/external reference values are `111.9649 deg` signed direction mean, `0.6747` anti-parallel rate, `0.1752` tmag median ratio, `0.1488` path ratio.
- TRAIN360C: self-developed hybrid spherical pose baseline with real training on the canonical train split.
- TRAIN360C vs T57b on test: signed direction mean `111.96 -> 54.96 deg`, anti-parallel rate `0.6747 -> 0.2157`, tmag median ratio `0.1752 -> 0.7693`, path ratio `0.1488 -> 0.5889`.
- BASE360C: HKUST official 360DVO external baseline with successful `cuda_ba` build/import, successful smoke test, and canonical val/test trajectory coverage `100%`.
- BASE360D: trajectory-derived pair-level component alignment of BASE360C against the canonical manifest; test all-pair metrics are `128.4026 deg` signed direction mean, `0.8149` anti-parallel rate, `0.0399` tmag median ratio, and `0.0435` pair-component path ratio.
- Current main caveats: TRAIN360C val/test discrepancy, BASE360D trajectory-derived partial comparability only, and official full-resolution image-directory OOM requiring a `0.5x` adapter.

## Caveat paragraph
Two caveats are central to interpreting the current results. First, `TRAIN360C` is a real and useful learned baseline, but it still shows a pronounced val/test discrepancy on signed translation direction: approximately `111.86 deg` on val versus `54.96 deg` on test. This means the baseline should be treated as a credible starting point rather than a fully stabilized endpoint. Second, `BASE360D` should not be over-interpreted as a native pairwise competitor to `TRAIN360C`. The official baseline is genuinely reproduced at the trajectory level, and pair-level metrics are now recovered through manifest-aligned post-processing, but those numbers are still trajectory-derived rather than direct image-pair forward outputs. Therefore, `BASE360D` should be interpreted as a trajectory-derived component-metric alignment of `BASE360C`, not as a direct image-pair forward baseline; the `0.5x` input adapter caveat must remain explicitly disclosed.
